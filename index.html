    Research

    How categories of identity become consequential, and how people live with, use and contest them.

    Doctoral research · ISEC, Bengaluru

    Aspiration as Orientation: Youth and Their Futures Through and Beyond Educational and Occupational Pathways

    Policy and research often read aspiration as a destination: a degree, a job, a rank. This study asks what that reading leaves out. It treats aspiration as an orientation toward a life a young person has reason to value, and follows how that orientation forms, how it relates to educational and occupational plans, and how social position shapes the ways it can be pursued.

    In revision

    The Negotiated–Embodied Caste: Mobility, Recognition and the Asymmetric Marking of Caste in Contemporary India

    A framework for why caste persists: it is embedded in bodies, perception and bureaucratic identity, yet that embeddedness is continually negotiated through mobility and state recognition.

    Under review

    The Two Archives

    How caste identity is held in two registers, the social and the documentary, and why documentary claims tend to move in one direction only. Draws on Kudmi-Mahato fieldwork.

    Under review

    Public reasoning about the legitimacy of protest

    A qualitative study of how members of the public judged whether the 2026 NEET protest in India was legitimate, read through online discourse.

    With Sagar Prasad (ISEC)
    In development

    Recognising the Other: a gateway mechanism of discrimination

    Why the same prejudiced person discriminates in one situation and not another. The paper argues that categorical identification of the other is a condition for prejudice to become practice, and plans classroom experiments to test it.
